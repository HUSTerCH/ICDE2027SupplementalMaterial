# Goal
Analyze the case narrative above in depth, locate the Awakening stage, and trace backward through the closing events of Transfer and Inducement. Identify the core combination of triggers that ended the scam and map them strictly to the predefined flat taxonomy of eight labels.

# Processing Logic
1. **Reverse Anchoring**:
   - Locate the reason for reporting the case or the trigger for recognition in the Awakening stage.
   - Always trace backward to the last few sentences of Transfer to identify the preceding objective event that triggered recognition.
   - **Absolute filter**: Ignore unsupported subjective intuitions, such as 'the more I thought about it, the angrier I became' or 'I felt uneasy.' Identify the substantive basis: physical/resource state (no money left), third-party actions (a police visit or being blocked by the scammer), information verification (asking at a bank), unmet expectations (the money never arriving), or shifting rules (a withdrawal error requiring another order).
2. **Mapping the underlying mechanism**: Assign the most precise category to the termination event strictly according to the label dictionary below. If multiple causes exist, such as running out of money plus a friend's intervention, output a label combination.

# Label Dictionary
1. <|System_Intervention|> - External system intervention: Public authorities or financial-institution systems proactively impose a block, such as an anti-scam alert, a call from 96110, a bank card locked by risk controls, or a police visit.
2. <|Social_Intervention|> - Close social intervention: Acquaintances such as family, friends, or colleagues intervene, or the victim recognizes the scam after proactively asking an acquaintance. Note: Verification with the actual person being impersonated belongs to external validation.
3. <|External_Validation|> - Proactive verification with an authoritative external party: After becoming suspicious, the victim checks with an authoritative channel, such as a bank counter, police station, or official customer service, or with the actual person being impersonated, and is explicitly told that it is a scam.
4. <|Fraud_Awareness_Trigger|> - Anti-scam awareness trigger: Recognition follows exposure to anti-scam content, such as finding similar scam cases online, watching an anti-scam program, seeing a platform warning, reading another victim's account of the same scam, or recalling earlier anti-scam education.
5. <|Internal_Logical|> - Breakdown of logical trust: The scammer remains in contact and continues inventing reasons to demand money, but the victim recognizes the absurdity through common sense, such as shifting rules or endless extra orders, actively refuses, and recognizes the scam. Excluded scenario: Merely discovering that funds have been moved without authorization.
6. <|Commitment_Failure|> - Failure of an expected commitment: The scammer promised a specific outcome, and the victim discovers that it cannot be fulfilled when trying to obtain it, such as being unable to withdraw, money not arriving, or goods differing from what was promised. This failure directly triggers recognition.
7. <|Attacker_Disengagement|> - Attacker disengagement/withdrawal: The scammer proactively cuts off contact, such as blocking the victim, dissolving a group, making the app inaccessible, or closing a store.
8. <|Objective_Termination|> - Objective interruption/passive termination: The process ends because of lost objective capacity or a passively discovered loss, such as exhausted funds, discovery of unauthorized deductions, or interruption of the physical communication channel.

# Commonly Confused Decisions
- Active recognition versus passive discovery: If the scammer is still attempting the scam and the victim recognizes and rejects it, assign <|Internal_Logical|>. Merely discovering that money has been moved belongs to <|Objective_Termination|>.
- Acquaintances versus institutions: Contacting an acquaintance belongs to <|Social_Intervention|>; going to a bank/police station or checking with the actual person being impersonated belongs to <|External_Validation|>.
- Proactive system intervention versus victim-initiated verification: Proactive interception by a system/institution belongs to <|System_Intervention|>; verification initiated by the victim belongs to <|External_Validation|>.
- If the narrative ends with only a statement that the victim 'previously received anti-scam publicity,' but recognition was actually triggered by another cause, **do not assign** <|Fraud_Awareness_Trigger|>.
- Identify the objective event preceding recognition and ignore purely subjective intuitions such as 'the more I thought about it, the angrier I became.'
