3. Rules for commonly confused scenarios:
    - Core rule for identity impersonation: There must be explicit impersonation of an identity that ordinary people generally trust, such as a public authority, acquaintance, celebrity or idol, existing or prospective business customer/supplier, a platform/customer-service representative backed by a major company or bank, or military, police, or fire-service personnel with an inherent status halo. The core purpose must be to obtain the victim's social trust. If the fabricated identity serves only to make the scam narrative plausible—for example, inventing a large customer's identity to justify a fictitious large order—rather than to gain social trust, do not classify it as identity impersonation.
    - Classifying impersonation of acquaintances/business partners: Whether the impersonated party is a personal acquaintance, such as a relative, friend, colleague, or superior, or a business partner, such as a supplier, customer, or contact at an enterprise or public institution, assign <|L2_Id_Acquaintance|> whenever the purpose is to abuse the victim's existing trust in that identity.
    - <|L2_Id_Gov|>: Forged official materials/channels versus verbal references to authority: Assign the label whenever materials or channels of the above agencies are forged, including documents, official websites, apps, official social-media accounts, or official text messages, regardless of purpose. Do not assign it when an agency's name is invoked only verbally to pressure the victim or collect money, such as saying 'the banking regulator will freeze your assets' or 'transfer to the banking regulator's account,' without forging such materials or channels.
    - <|L2_Tran_Shop|> versus <|L2_Tran_Procurement|>: Use the former when an individual consumer or second-hand seller is scammed while trying to buy fictitious goods/services or sell personal unused items. Use the latter when an operating business is deceived by an impersonated large customer. In a B2B fake-procurement scenario, also output <|L2_Id_Acquaintance|> if the scammer's identity is supported by ongoing trust-building actions, such as providing credentials, business cards, or contracts, or if the parties already had a business relationship. Do not add an identity-impersonation label for a merely ad hoc, fabricated large-customer identity. Prioritize <|L2_Tran_Game|> for all scams involving online-game virtual goods, whether the victim is the buyer or seller.
    - Do not upgrade extraction within a task-rebate chain to a financial label: In task-rebate scams, demands for further transfers because of 'operation errors,' 'frozen accounts,' 'linked or stuck orders,' or 'insufficient transaction volume' are inherent parts of <|L2_Bait_Task|> and must not be upgraded to finance-related labels. Consider adding a financial label only when the scammer leads the victim away from the original task-rebate platform to a completely new, independent investment platform and carries out an investment scam.
    - Strict definition of fake fund liquidation/refunds: <|L2_Fin_Recovery|> applies to secondary scams targeting people who have genuinely suffered financial losses, such as a collapsed P2P lending platform, a closed online-course provider or gym, or previous investment losses, with fund liquidation or refund terminology appearing in the narrative. Do not use this label when a consumer is deceived by an impersonated customer-service representative offering compensation for logistics or product-quality problems.
    - Distinguishing social engineering from technical theft:
        * Excluded: Do not assign this label when the victim completes the transfer, such as voluntarily scanning a code or entering the amount/password, or voluntarily discloses a verification code for a single transfer. These are deliberately authorized transfers induced by social engineering.
        * Included: The scammer must obtain underlying control and independently complete a transfer without the victim's deliberate authorization. This includes: 1. Account-level takeover: obtaining an Apple ID, online-banking credentials, or verification codes for logging in to a financial app, resetting a password, or linking a digital wallet, then logging in remotely to take over the account; 2. Remote control/screen-sharing credential theft: covertly viewing verification codes/passwords through screen sharing or directly controlling the mouse to make transfers; 3. Technical compromise: silently intercepting text messages or controlling devices through malware or similar means.
        * Simple test: It qualifies if the scammer can continue stealing funds without the victim supplying further verification codes; otherwise, it does not.
    - Distinguishing romance/dating from sex-related baiting: <|L2_Soc_Sex|> focuses on immediate sexual desire. Even if described euphemistically as 'local dating,' classify it as sex-related baiting whenever an offline meeting is proposed within a very short period, such as the same day. <|L2_Soc_Romance|> focuses on developing a long-term emotional relationship and building trust through sustained companionship.
