# Awakening-Factor Taxonomy

## 1. Classification Scope

This taxonomy records events of scam recognition or termination that actually occur in a case, and the factors that trigger them. Its 8 categories distinguish proactive intervention by external actors, verification initiated by victims, anomalies within the scam process, and objective conditions that prevent further interaction.

The Awakening stage covers both recognizing the scam and the cessation of further payments. Some labels therefore describe events in which the victim has recognized the scam, while others describe interruptions to funds or interaction. Resource exhaustion, discovery of a technical transfer, or attacker disengagement does not necessarily mean that the victim had already formed a complete understanding of the scam.

Labels apply to the whole case and may be combined, with independent triggering evidence for each. Analysis examines Awakening and also traces backward to the final events of Transfer and Inducement to establish why payments stopped or recognition occurred at that point.

## 2. Category Overview

| Category | Label code | Core trigger |
|---|---|---|
| External System Intervention | `System_Intervention` | Police, banks, or other institutional systems proactively warn, intercept, or intervene. |
| Close Social Intervention | `Social_Intervention` | Family, friends, colleagues, or other acquaintances discourage participation, or the victim recognizes the scam after asking them. |
| Proactive External Validation | `External_Validation` | The victim proactively verifies claims with an authoritative channel or the actual party being impersonated and learns that it is a scam. |
| Anti-Scam Awareness Trigger | `Fraud_Awareness_Trigger` | Existing anti-scam knowledge, independently consulted information, or similar cases help the victim recognize the scam. |
| Internal Logical Breakdown | `Internal_Logical` | The victim identifies contradictions or absurd demands in the narrative, loses trust, and refuses to continue. |
| Commitment Failure | `Commitment_Failure` | A specific promise cannot be fulfilled, and this failure directly triggers recognition. |
| Attacker Disengagement | `Attacker_Disengagement` | The scammer proactively cuts contact, blocks the victim, dissolves a group, closes a platform, or otherwise withdraws, leading to termination or recognition. |
| Objective Termination | `Objective_Termination` | Exhausted funds, discovery of unauthorized transfers, interruption of physical communication, or similar conditions passively end the process. |

## 3. Detailed Descriptions of the Eight Categories

### 3.1 External System Intervention: `System_Intervention`

**Definition.** Public authorities or financial, telecommunications, payment, or other institutional systems proactively warn, block, or intervene, causing recognition or stopping further payments.

**Typical evidence.** Anti-scam alerts, unsolicited police calls or visits, banks proactively checking unusual transfers, cards locked by risk controls, or blocked transactions.

**Decision boundaries.** A system includes both automated risk controls and human intervention by institutional staff or police. The key is that an external institution proactively identifies risk and acts. A victim visiting a bank or police station to verify a claim belongs to Proactive External Validation; the involvement of a bank or police does not itself make it System Intervention.

### 3.2 Close Social Intervention: `Social_Intervention`

**Definition.** Family, friends, colleagues, or others in the victim's real-world social network intervene, or the victim proactively asks them and recognizes the scam after their warning.

**Typical evidence.** A family member stops an unusual payment, a colleague identifies the described situation as a scam, or a friend helps the victim recognize the scam after hearing the account.

**Decision boundaries.** Contacting an acquaintance may be proactive help seeking, but that person's role here is to provide a warning or persuasion as an acquaintance. If the victim contacts the actual person being impersonated to verify an identity or request, use Proactive External Validation.

### 3.3 Proactive External Validation: `External_Validation`

**Definition.** After becoming uncertain, the victim proactively checks with an authoritative channel or the actual person being impersonated and recognizes the scam through explicit external confirmation.

**Typical evidence.** Visiting a bank counter, calling official customer service, asking at a police station, or directly contacting the real superior, relative, friend, institution, or other impersonated party to confirm that the request was not genuinely made by them.

**Decision boundaries.** There must be a record of actual verification and confirmation. Wanting to ask, planning to verify, or merely seeking help is not equivalent to recognition already triggered by verification. This category differs from System Intervention because the victim initiates the check, and from Close Social Intervention in the target and function of the verification.

### 3.4 Anti-Scam Awareness Trigger: `Fraud_Awareness_Trigger`

**Definition.** Exposure to, recall of, or an independent search for anti-scam information helps the victim reinterpret the current experience and recognize the scam.

**Typical evidence.** Finding similar scam cases through a search, seeing an anti-scam program or platform warning, encountering accounts from victims of similar cases, or recalling earlier anti-scam education.

**Decision boundaries.** Do not assign the label merely because the case says that the victim previously received anti-scam publicity, without showing that this knowledge contributed to recognition. Distinguish the actual role of the warning: knowledge or similar cases triggering recognition belong here, while proactive institutional interruption of the current case is assessed as System Intervention.

### 3.5 Internal Logical Breakdown: `Internal_Logical`

**Definition.** While the scammer remains in contact and invents reasons to request money, the victim uses common sense to identify contradictions or absurd demands, actively refuses, and recognizes the scam. The original prompt also calls this a breakdown of logical trust.

**Typical evidence.** Rules changing after each requirement is satisfied, endless demands for additional orders, fees required to recover earlier payments, or other contradictions that reveal that the explanations cannot hold.

**Decision boundaries.** Identify the specific basis for the collapse of trust. Intuitions such as becoming angrier when thinking about it or feeling uneasy, without factual support, do not independently justify this label. Merely discovering that money has been moved from a bank account is passive discovery of loss and does not by itself establish logical recognition.

### 3.6 Commitment Failure: `Commitment_Failure`

**Definition.** The scammer makes a specific promise of an outcome. When attempting to obtain it, the victim discovers that it cannot be fulfilled and consequently recognizes the scam.

**Typical evidence.** Promised funds do not arrive, a promised withdrawal cannot be made, goods never arrive, or delivered goods differ substantially from the promise, with the narrative explicitly connecting the failure to recognition.

**Decision boundaries.** A promise existing or remaining unfulfilled is not automatically an awakening factor. Confirm that its failure actually triggered recognition. If recognition mainly follows blocking by the scammer or closure of the platform, an accompanying withdrawal-under-review status does not automatically add this label.

### 3.7 Attacker Disengagement: `Attacker_Disengagement`

**Definition.** The scammer proactively stops contact or withdraws, preventing further interaction and leading to cessation of payments or recognition.

**Typical evidence.** Being blocked, all contact channels becoming unavailable, a scam group being dissolved, a store being closed, or a platform shutting down or becoming inaccessible.

**Decision boundaries.** Confirm the withdrawal or loss-of-contact event from the narrative, distinguishing it from ordinary device or network failures. If the scammer immediately cuts contact after obtaining the money, that disconnection is an independent trigger. Do not infer a separate Commitment Failure solely because no withdrawal occurred during the same period.

### 3.8 Objective Termination: `Objective_Termination`

**Definition.** The victim loses the objective ability to pay or the conditions needed to continue interaction, or passively discovers unauthorized loss, bringing the scam process to a stop.

**Typical evidence.** Funds are exhausted and further borrowing is unavailable, unauthorized deductions or transfers are discovered, or a device or physical communication channel is interrupted.

**Decision boundaries.** Financial difficulty must be linked to an actual inability to continue paying; economic background alone is insufficient. Payments may stop before full recognition: the victim may still trust the other party but be unable to obtain the next payment. If the victim subsequently initiates police verification and learns that it is a scam, both independent mechanisms may be recorded.

## 4. Key Decision Boundaries

| Commonly confused categories | Decision question |
|---|---|
| System Intervention and Proactive External Validation | Does the institution proactively contact, warn, or block, or does the victim initiate verification? |
| Close Social Intervention and Proactive External Validation | Does the acquaintance provide persuasion or a warning, or confirm that an identity or request is false as the actual person being impersonated? |
| Anti-Scam Awareness Trigger and System Intervention | Does anti-scam information help the victim recognize a pattern, or does an external system proactively act on the current case? |
| Internal Logical Breakdown and Commitment Failure | Does the victim identify contradictory explanations or demands, or does a specific unfulfilled promise directly trigger recognition? |
| Internal Logical Breakdown and Objective Termination | Does the victim actively recognize and refuse, or merely discover a transfer or become objectively unable to continue paying? |
| Attacker Disengagement and Commitment Failure | Does proactive loss of contact or platform closure trigger recognition, or does the failure to obtain a promised outcome do so? |

Multiple labels are allowed when independent triggers occur in the same case. The distinctions above do not make every pair of categories mutually exclusive.

## 5. Evidence and Multiple-Label Principles

1. Locate the final recognition, refusal, help-seeking, or payment-cessation event to establish how the case ends.
2. Trace backward through payment demands, transfers, verification, and communication changes to identify the actual trigger.
3. Distinguish events that occurred from the victim's plans, guesses, or background information.
4. Require independent evidence for each label. Do not count the same event as multiple mechanisms by merely rephrasing it.
5. If the narrative states only that the victim recognized the scam and reported it, do not infer the awakening category from reporting alone; examine the preceding concrete events.

For example, a victim whose loan application is rejected may be unable to continue paying, then visit a police station and learn that it is a scam. Both `Objective_Termination` and `External_Validation` may be recorded, representing interruption of payment capacity and confirmation through proactive verification, respectively.

If recognition follows blocking by the scammer and platform closure, a preceding pending withdrawal alone does not justify adding `Commitment_Failure`. Additional evidence must show that the unfulfilled promise was itself an independent recognition trigger.

## 6. Label Representation and Corresponding Prompts

The fine-tuning version uses special tokens such as `<|External_Validation|>`, while the baseline version uses ordinary text forms such as `<External_Validation>`. Label names and meanings do not change with their representation.

Task outputs contain an analysis and a label array, for example:

```json
{
  "analysis": "Further funds could not be raised, after which the victim proactively checked with police and confirmed the scam.",
  "labels": [
    "<Objective_Termination>",
    "<External_Validation>"
  ]
}
```
